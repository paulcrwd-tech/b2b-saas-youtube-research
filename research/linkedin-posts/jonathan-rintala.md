> When we started Univid, every customer was Swedish.

Today, roughly 30% of our customers come from outside Sweden - across the US, Europe, Australia, and beyond.

But we never hired country managers.
We never opened local offices.
Never ran targeted outbound or paid ads.

Instead, we focused on one thing: a razor-sharp ICP.

Then we spent years improving the product for them, helping these customers succeed, and letting inbound do the heavy lifting to get more of the same people in the door.

The more specific we became about who we serve, the more international our customer base became.

A good reminder that global SaaS companies aren't built country by country.

They're built customer by customer.

And we are just getting started! 🚀

**Why relevant:** Univid grew to 30% international customers (US, Europe, Australia) with zero country managers, local offices, or paid/targeted ads, purely by sharpening their ICP and letting product-led inbound compound — supporting the thesis that international SaaS expansion can be driven by narrowing who you serve rather than by geographic expansion tactics.

>People are often surprised when I tell them our GTM team at Univid is 1 FTE (pictured).

We grow 95% through an organic inbound content play (SEO/AIO).

But it wasn't a quick AI hack or overnight SEO win to get here. It took years of building structured assets that compound.

Instead of spending time chasing cold prospects or slow deals, we spend it creating valuable content:

- webinar statistics reports
- how to YT videos with real examples
- webinar success stories from real marketers
- free marketing tools

Content that is so good that people (and AI) actually link back or refer peers to it.

Combined with relevant growth hooks to a product that is dead simple to get started with.

Result = a crazy lean GTM machine.

And it works. I've personally closed 200+ B2B SaaS deals this way.

And no - the answer is definitely not to automate all your content with AI. Despite what you might read on LinkedIn.

**Why relevant:**Useful for the playbook as a strategic caveat on the "vibe coding" trend: it reframes the value proposition away from cost savings (which don't materialize once you count soft costs) toward bu Univid runs its entire go-to-market with 1 FTE and grows 95% through organic inbound (SEO/AIO), built over years via compounding content assets (statistics reports, how-to videos, case studies, free tools) rather than paid ads or SDR outbound — with the founder personally closing 200+ B2B SaaS deals this way, and an explicit counter-point that the win came from content quality/linkability over years, not from mass-producing content with AI.
